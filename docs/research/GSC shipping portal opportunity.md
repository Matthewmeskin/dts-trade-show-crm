# Independent decorators need freight help, not software

The need is real, but it is narrower than "GSCs want a shipping portal." Freeman, GES, Shepard and The Expo Group already run their own logistics arms and exhibitor portals, so they are competitors, not customers. The opening sits with the regional and local decorators who have no logistics arm and still hand exhibitors a PDF kit, a fax number and "email us for a quote." For those shops, the freight problem that costs everybody money is not the linehaul. It is the missed warehouse cutoff, the unlabeled crate, and above all the outbound shipment with no carrier and no MHA at move out, which turns into forced freight, floor clearing headaches and billing disputes. A show branded portal where exhibitors book inbound and outbound, print labels and BOLs, file outbound paperwork and see status, with DTS coordinating the carriers behind it, gives an independent decorator the same kit the majors sell. What the decorator values most is a clean dock and a clean floor: a manifest before the show, freight that shows up labeled for the right booth, and outbound booked before close. Revenue share matters, but it comes second, and it has to be structured around the federal rule that bars brokers from giving anything of value to a shipper or consignee. DTS should build the outbound and manifest pieces first, prove them at one or two shows with a friendly independent, and treat the payment model as a legal question before it becomes a sales pitch.

## Drayage pays the bills, and transportation is the leg anyone can win

A GSC makes its money on exhibitor services, and material handling is the big one. Organizers award the GSC an exclusive contract and push show costs onto it, and the GSC earns that back by billing exhibitors for drayage, labor, rentals and electrical ([Exponents](https://www.exponents.com/our-blog/making-sense-of-the-major-players-involved-in-producing-trade-shows/)). Show managers often ask the GSC to roll carpet, signage and registration costs into drayage so the organizer's own bill nets to zero, which is one documented reason drayage runs so high ([EXHIBITOR Magazine, 2016](https://www.exhibitoronline.com/topics/article.asp?ID=2053)). The same source found **material handling rose 257% from 1996 to 2013 while installation labor rose 21%**. The climb has not stopped: the Exhibitor Advocate's survey of 224 exhibitor manuals put the **2025 average material handling rate at $2.28 per pound, up 9.5% in one year and 21.3% since 2022** ([The Exhibitor Advocate](https://www.exhibitoradvocacy.com/blog/the-exhibitor-advocate-releases-2025-annual-survey-of-exhibition-rates)). Freeman shows now quote flat per pound rates like $2.50 at AHR Expo 2027 ([AHR Expo manual](https://www.ahrexhibitormanual.com/shipping-overview)).

The line that matters for DTS is where material handling stops and transportation starts. Material handling covers the move from the carrier's trailer to the booth, empty storage and the trip back to the dock, and the GSC holds it exclusively ([GES](https://insights.ges.com/us-exhibitor-roadmap/trade-show-freight-shipping-material-handling); [Freeman](https://www.freeman.com/resources/quick-guide-the-international-exhibitor/)). Transportation, the over the road move to and from the venue, is open: exhibitors can use any carrier ([DTS](https://www.dtsone.com/trade-show-shipping-key-players/)). So the GSC owns the dock, the paperwork and the clock, and the trucking is up for grabs. That is why the majors built their own freight brands and why an independent without one is leaving money and control on the table.

The majors pull transportation in house with a simple lever: discount the drayage they control. Freeman's Roundtrip Ready takes **10% off both shipping and material handling** when the exhibitor books both legs with Freeman ([Freeman](https://www.freeman.com/event-services/freeman-transportation/)). GES Transportation+ gives 10% off material handling, a free weight certificate and no carrier wait time fees on one invoice ([GES](https://www.ges.com/exhibitor/shipping-logistics/)). Shepard waives late fees and throws in priority empty return and outbound shrink wrap ([Shepard flyer](https://aimexpousa.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Shepard-Logistics.pdf)). The Expo Group promises no inbound special handling, no marshalling yard detention and "no forced outbound freight" ([The Expo Group](https://theexpogroup.com/expogistics/)). Every one of those perks is paid for out of material handling, which a third party cannot touch. Historically the GSC also earned from forced freight through a "loading allowance" profit share with the official carrier ([EXHIBITOR Magazine](https://www.exhibitoronline.com/topics/article.asp?ID=1365)), and official contractors "often have rebate agreements" with organizers ([EXHIBITOR Online](https://www.exhibitoronline.com/topics/article.asp?ID=877)). No public source puts a current number on either, and nobody publishes what share of GSC revenue or margin comes from material handling. For scale, GES reported **$888.4 million in 2023 revenue and about a 7.7% adjusted EBITDA margin** before its sale to private equity ([TSNN](https://www.tsnn.com/trade-show-marketing/earnings-ges-and-spiro-parent-company-viad-positive-on-2024-after-double-digit-revenue-growth-in-q4-2023)), which suggests thin company margins that lean on the exhibitor service lines.

## Forced freight at move out is where exhibitors and decorators both bleed

Almost every freight problem at a show is a missed date, a missing document or a bad label, not a truck breaking down. SourceOne's list of top exhibitor mistakes reads like a service desk log: shipping late, missing or stale labels, no inventory, forgetting the return trip, and nobody clearly owning the job ([SourceOne Events](https://sourceoneevents.com/resource/top-5-logistics-mistakes-exhibitors-make-before-a-trade-show/)). GES's own first timer sheet warns about carriers that don't know trade shows, old labels left on crates and bad piece counts ([GES](https://ordering.ges.com/011600388/download/359161)). Each mistake carries a surcharge. One GES order form set a **30% late to warehouse surcharge, a 30% mixed shipment surcharge and a 60% uncrated surcharge** ([GES order form](https://ordering.ges.com/071600793/download/858356)), and overtime and off target fees stack on top.

Forced freight is the worst of it. When the exhibitor has no completed Material Handling Agreement or the carrier misses its check in at the marshalling yard, the GSC reroutes the shipment "on Freeman's carrier choice at the exhibitor's expense," as the JCK manual puts it ([JCK manual](https://lasvegas.jckonline.com/content/dam/sitebuilder/rna/jck/las-vegas/2023/docs/exhibitor-manual/JCKLV23-Ops-ExMan-Vndr-GC-MaterialHandlingBrochure-Form.pdf.coredownload.788985671.pdf)). Exhibitors then pay extra handling, local cartage, off target and overtime fees, and "undiscounted or highly inflated" carrier rates, and rerouting can take weeks, so freight misses the next show ([EXHIBITOR Magazine](https://www.exhibitoronline.com/topics/article.asp?ID=1865&catID=91)). Candy Adams, writing in EXHIBITOR, said "a ridiculous amount of freight gets forced back to the GSC," and listed wrong convention centers, wrong hotels and drivers with no marshalling yard address as regular causes ([EXHIBITOR Magazine](https://www.exhibitoronline.com/topics/article.asp?ID=2569)). Move out makes it worse because the window is short and every exhibitor is shopping for a truck at the same hour ([Freightzy](https://www.freightzy.com/blog/trade-show-shipping-costs-freight-drayage-full-budget-breakdown)).

Exhibitors are fed up with cost in general. **80% name cost management as their top challenge and 64% are scaling back their show presence** ([Exhibit City News](https://exhibitcitynews.com/the-numbers-dont-lie-neither-do-exhibitors/)), and only 39% rate organizer support as very effective ([PCMA](https://www.pcma.org/help-out-event-exhibitors/)). A portal cannot lower the decorator's base drayage rate, and DTS should not pretend it can. What it can go after is the avoidable money: the late, off target, uncrated and forced freight charges that come from missed steps.

The decorator side of the pain is thinner on evidence but easy to see. Forced freight means sorting, staging and rerouting labor, billing disputes and angry exhibitors, and the GSC is on the hook to hand the hall back on time. Organizers now use rate surveys to push back on GSCs in contract talks ([TSNN](https://www.tsnn.com/trade-shows-conferences/annual-report-sheds-light-on-exhibition-costs-and-pricing-trends)). The strongest signal is that Freeman built this into its new exhibitor platform in December 2025, with **self service MHA submission and "real time visibility into shipping, handling, and invoice status"** because, in its EVP's words, clients "wanted a simpler, more straightforward way to get ready" ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/12/09/3202564/0/en/Freeman-Launches-New-Exhibitor-Services-Platform-to-Make-Exhibiting-Easier.html)). The biggest decorator in the country decided shipping visibility and outbound paperwork were worth building. That validates the problem and sets the bar independents now get compared against. One honest caveat: nobody publishes how often freight gets forced at a typical show or how many service desk calls are about shipping, so DTS will need to get those numbers from decorators directly.

## Freight is the least built out part of every decorator's tech, and independents run on PDFs

Between 2023 and 2026 the big GSCs all relaunched their exhibitor portals: The Expo Group's ExpoPortal in June 2025, Shepard's ExhibitorPro replacing its Online Kit, and Freeman's new platform rolling out in 2026 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-expo-group-redefines-trade-show-exhibitor-experience-with-brand-new-expoportal-302489072.html); [Shepard](https://shepardexhibitorpro.com/exclusive-preview/)). The focus is shopping carts, checklists and invoices. Freight lags even there. GES's Expresso site offers label printing, a quote request and 24/7 tracking, but no BOL generator or pickup scheduling was visible ([Expresso by GES](https://ordering.ges.com/022601847/shipping)). Shepard booked logistics through a PDF form emailed to its logistics desk and tells exhibitors to get tracking from their carrier ([Shepard form](https://www.fastenerfairusa.com/content/dam/sitebuilder/rna/fastener-fair/2023/docs/exhibitor-manual-2023/17-Logistics-Order-Form.pdf.coredownload.760592910.pdf); [Shepard FAQ](https://shepardes.com/faq/shipping-material-handling/where-can-i-find-tracking-information-for-my-shipments/)). Fern's OneView portal handles ordering, deadlines and invoices, with no freight tools mentioned ([Fern](https://fernexpo.com/services/exhibitor-services/)).

Below that tier it gets manual fast. Heritage's 2025 ASGCT kit takes orders online, by email, fax or phone, ships BOLs and labels inside the PDF, and quotes freight by email ([ASGCT 2025 kit](https://support.annualmeeting.asgct.org/asgct2025/CUSTOM/images/2025%20Exhibitor%20Services%20Kit.pdf)). Capital Convention Contractors printed freight labels in the manual, hands out outbound BOLs at the service desk, and names ABF as official carrier by phone number ([IWCS 2025 manual](https://www.iwcs.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/IWCS-2025-Supplier-Exhibition-Exhibitor-Service-Manual.pdf)). Hale Expo Services takes orders by mail, fax or scanned forms ([WNY FM Expo](https://www.fmexpo.net/exhibitor-forms)). Convention center house decorators in Palm Beach and Savannah still send PDF kits ([PBCCC kit](https://ctaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/PBCCC-Exhibitor-Ordering-Kit.pdf); [Savannah kit](https://www.savconventioncenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/SCC-Exhibitor-Ordering-Kit-2025.pdf)).

The software that independents buy does not fill the gap. Boomer Commerce sells branded storefronts and order management to smaller contractors (Heritage uses it), and ConventionSuite sells a NetSuite based ERP to decorators and exhibit houses, but neither advertises freight booking ([Boomer Commerce](https://boomercommerce.com/); [ConventionSuite](https://conventionsuite.com/exhibit-house-software/)). Organizer platforms like Map Your Show stop at a checklist and a link to the GSC ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/map-your-show-llc-announces-upgrades-to-exhibitor-resource-center-technology-301885259.html)). Nobody reviewed offers an independent decorator a packaged flow from quote to BOL to pickup to tracking. That is the slot. It also means DTS should plan to plug into Boomer or a decorator's existing store with a link or embed rather than ask a decorator to rip anything out.

## The majors and a few specialist carriers already run this playbook

The competition sorts into four groups, and each one tells DTS something different.

| Who | Examples | What they offer exhibitors | What it means for DTS |
|---|---|---|---|
| GSC logistics arms | Freeman Transportation, GES Transportation+, Shepard Logistics, Expogistics, HES Logistics | Quotes, labels, tracking, one invoice, 10% drayage discounts, waived fees | Cannot be matched on drayage perks; these GSCs are rivals, not buyers |
| Specialist show carriers | BTX, EFW (Estes), Rock-It Cargo, Sho-Air | Official carrier status, show portals, prelabeled freight, onsite desks | Closest model to what DTS would become |
| LTL and parcel programs | UPS Trade Show, ArcBest, XPO, ODFL | Trade show service desks, tracking, electronic BOLs | Capacity DTS can tap, and competitors for the same exhibitor |
| Digital freight platforms | FreightCenter, Freightquote, uShip | Instant LTL quotes and booking | Skip the show layer entirely; DTS can differentiate there |

BTX is the example to study. It was named **Official Ground/Air Freight Carrier for IAEE's Expo! Expo! on a three year deal (2025 to 2027)**, with exact per pound quotes, preprinted labels delivered to the booth, an online portal for quote, booking and tracking, an onsite desk during move in and move out, and a 10% round trip discount ([Exhibit City News](https://exhibitcitynews.com/btx-global-logistics-named-official-expo-expo-ground-air-freight-carrier/)). EFW claims relationships "with every major venue and show decorator" and staff at major events ([EFW](https://efwnow.com/tradeshow/)). At the other end, FreightCenter spells out that it "does not control the trade show's drayage or material-handling charges" ([FreightCenter](https://www.freightcenter.com/services/trade-show-freight-shipping/)), which is exactly why generic platforms cannot handle booth numbers, targets, marshalling yards or MHAs.

No source turned up a third party broker running a white label shipping portal under a decorator's brand. The nearest cousin is PartnerShip, which runs co branded shipping programs for hundreds of trade associations and pitches them as non dues revenue ([PartnerShip](https://www.partnership.com/shipping-services/build-association-shipping-program)). That model, a branded program the partner promotes while the logistics company does the work, is the one DTS would be bringing to decorators. The large GSCs will not buy it. Fern and Heritage are gray areas, since Fern handles 1,400 plus events a year and Heritage already runs HES Logistics ([ESCA directory](https://member.esca.org/); [Heritage](https://www.heritagesvs.com/)). The cleanest targets are the regional and house decorators with no freight arm, which ESCA's directory of roughly 150 plus visible members is a starting list for.

## Decorators want a clean floor first and a revenue share second

The things GSCs sell to exhibitors through their own freight arms show what they care about: no forced outbound, no marshalling yard detention, correct labels, priority empty return ([The Expo Group](https://theexpogroup.com/expogistics/); [Shepard](https://aimexpousa.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Shepard-Logistics.pdf)). Freeman requires an MHA "before leaving the event, regardless of carrier," and GES wants booth number, venue and contact on every BOL ([JCK 2025 manual](https://lasvegas.jckonline.com/content/dam/sitebuilder/rna/jck/las-vegas/2025/exhibitor-manual/JCKLV25-Ops-ExMan-Vndr-GC-Freight%20Process.pdf.coredownload.344545126.pdf); [GES](https://insights.ges.com/us-exhibitor-roadmap/trade-show-shipping)). Smaller GSCs also compete on service and relationships rather than price ([Exhibit City News](https://exhibitcitynews.com/shop-to-showfloor-bringing-the-service-to-general-service-contractors/)). Reading that together, the likely order of what an independent decorator values is: a manifest of piece counts, weights, carriers and ETAs by booth before move in; freight that arrives with the right labels; outbound booked and MHA data in hand before close; a share of the freight money; and keeping its own brand in front of the exhibitor. That order is a judgment from the evidence, not survey data, and the interviews below should test it.

On money, the documented pattern for official contractors is status, exhibitor list and an onsite desk in exchange for exhibitor discounts and sometimes a profit split or rebate to show management ([EXHIBITOR Online](https://www.exhibitoronline.com/topics/article.asp?ID=877)). No public figures exist for freight. The closest benchmark is association affinity programs, where retail royalties run **2% to 10% of purchases** ([i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/member-benefits-partnerships/)). DTS should model a few rates rather than lean on a norm that does not exist.

The legal problem sits right in the middle of the pitch. **49 CFR 371.9(b) says a broker "shall not give or offer to give anything of value to any shipper, consignor or consignee"** beyond small promotional items ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-371/subpart-A/section-371.9)). Exhibitors are the shippers, so nothing can flow back to them. The decorator is trickier: advance warehouse freight is usually addressed in care of the GSC's warehouse, which arguably makes the decorator a consignee. The research found no FMCSA guidance or case law on brokers paying referral or licensing fees to a GSC or organizer. That leaves three structures worth putting in front of transportation counsel. The first pays the organizer, who is not the shipper or consignee, a sponsorship or licensing fee for official carrier status, the same way BTX got its designation. The second has the decorator fund any exhibitor discount out of its own drayage, the way Freeman and GES do, so DTS pays nothing to the decorator and the decorator's benefit is the cleaner floor plus more exhibitors using its bundle. The third charges the decorator a flat technology or service fee, or waives one, so value flows through operations rather than cash back to a consignee. Any of these beats a per shipment kickback to the decorator, which is the version most likely to draw scrutiny. Separately, FMCSA's broker transparency rulemaking is still a proposal, with a final rule unlikely before early 2027 per a secondary source, so DTS should build electronic transaction records into the portal from day one ([FMCSA docket](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/docket-no-fmcsa-2023-0257-rin-2126-ac63-transparency-property-broker-transactions); [Innovative Logistics Group](https://cms.innovativelogisticsgroup.io/business-fundamentals/fmcsa-second-broker-transparency-rulemaking-lands-in-may-2026-with-48-hour-records-mandate-how-small-carriers-use-the-new-49-cfr-371-power-to-recover-margin/)).

## What DTS should build first, and how to prove it

Position the product as the decorator's own freight program, not as DTS's website with the decorator's logo on it. The pitch in plain terms: "Your exhibitors book their freight on your site, with your show's dates, addresses and labels baked in. We coordinate the trucks, chase the paperwork and tell you what is coming to your dock and when. You get a manifest before move in and outbound locked down before close." DTS is the broker and says so on every page: it arranges and coordinates, carriers haul, and carrier liability limits apply unless the exhibitor buys cargo coverage, which matters because released value can be as low as $0.30 to $0.60 per pound ([EXHIBITOR Magazine](https://www.exhibitoronline.com/topics/article.asp?ID=2569)). Do not promise on time delivery or no forced freight. Promise that outbound is booked, the MHA data is filled out, the carrier has the check in time, and somebody is watching.

The build order should follow the money and the pain, which both point to outbound.

| Phase | Feature | Why it goes here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Show setup from the exhibitor manual: advance warehouse and show site addresses, receiving windows, targets, marshalling yard address, move out check in deadline | Every surcharge traces back to a date or address the exhibitor missed |
| 1 | Outbound booking with MHA data capture (carrier, contact, 24 hour phone, destination, piece count), reminders before close, and a manifest by booth for the decorator | Goes straight at forced freight, the top pain for both sides |
| 1 | Inbound booking with auto generated labels (show, booth, warehouse or direct, piece X of Y) and BOL | Fixes the stale label and wrong address problems |
| 1 | Pre show manifest for the decorator: booths, pieces, weights, carriers, ETAs | The thing a decorator can use at the dock that no generic freight site gives it |
| 2 | Card payment, round trip booking, optional cargo insurance, status updates by email or text | Matches what exhibitors now expect from Freeman and GES |
| 2 | Landed cost estimate: linehaul plus the decorator's own drayage rate plus likely surcharges, clearly split between DTS charges and GSC charges | Answers the sticker shock complaint without quoting charges DTS does not control |
| 3 | Links or embeds into Boomer and other decorator stores, and an organizer facing view | Distribution without asking decorators to change systems |

Instant multi carrier LTL pricing is nice to have but not the wedge; exhibitors can already get LTL quotes online. The show layer is what nobody else packages for independents.

The risks are real. Decorators with any freight arm will see DTS as a competitor, and even those without one may earn money on forced freight today, so "less forced freight" has to be sold as labor saved and a hall cleared on time, not lost revenue. Organizers can still appoint someone else as official carrier, and show rules let management override an exhibitor's carrier after a missed pickup while disclaiming liability ([Law Insider](https://lawinsider.com/clause/designated-carriers)). Volume per show at a small regional event may be too thin to pay for setup, so the per show configuration has to be quick. And much of the tactical advice in this space comes from forwarders and exhibit houses with something to sell, so the pain is well documented but not measured.

Validation should happen before much code gets written. Pull 20 to 30 regional and house decorators from the ESCA directory and from exhibitor manuals that still say "email for a quote" or list a single official LTL carrier, and get 10 to 15 conversations with the owner or ops manager. Ask how many shipments were forced at their last three shows, how many service desk hours go to freight questions, who they send exhibitors to for trucking now and whether they get paid for it, whether they would put their name on a freight program, and what would make them say no. Then pitch one friendly decorator on a free pilot at one or two mid size shows, with DTS doing the manual work behind a simple portal if needed. Measure the share of exhibitors who book through it, the share of outbound booked and MHA complete before close, forced freight count against the decorator's usual, service desk freight calls, and DTS margin per show. If a pilot decorator will not share those numbers or will not promote the portal in its kit, that is the answer too.

## Conclusion

The takeaway is that the product is really a service with a front end. Freeman spent real money to give exhibitors self service MHAs and shipping status, and independents cannot match that on their own, but what an independent is actually buying from DTS is a coordinator who owns the exhibitor freight headache end to end under the decorator's name. The portal is how that coordination scales past a few shows. That favors DTS, since a broker's core skill is exactly this kind of chasing, confirming and communicating, and it means early pilots can lean on people before the software is finished.

The bigger strategic point is that official carrier status, not the portal, is the asset that locks in freight. BTX got it from an organizer on a three year deal. If the 371.9 question pushes payments toward the organizer rather than the decorator, DTS may find the best customer is a pair: an independent decorator who wants the tools and an organizer who awards the designation. Winning both at a handful of regional shows would give DTS a repeatable playbook that the big GSCs have no reason to offer anyone outside their own house.
